What changed from your build

PNY RTX 5080 Epic-X → Gigabyte RTX 5080 Aero OC (white)

−$34

Same GPU chip and speed, but white to match the case, board, RAM and cooler. Gigabyte’s Aero cooler has three fans and a vapor chamber, and at 304 mm it leaves lots of room in the case.

Crucial T500 2TB → WD_Black SN7100 2TB

−$107

Same class of fast PCIe 4.0 drive. The T500 was $409 and nearly sold out; the SN7100 is $302 and in stock.

Corsair iCUE LINK QX120 → Arctic P12 Pro White A-RGB (reverse blade, 3-pack)

−$54

FIX iCUE LINK fans only run through Corsair’s iCUE LINK System Hub, which is not in the build. The Arctic fans plug straight into the motherboard and match the Arctic cooler’s lighting. Reverse blades are made for the case floor, so the clean side faces the glass.

Thermal Grizzly Conductonaut Extreme → Arctic MX-6

−$40

FIX Conductonaut is liquid metal. It conducts electricity, can short the motherboard if a drop escapes, and is meant for experienced overclockers. MX-6 is safe and does not dry out for years.

The CPU, board, RAM, cooler, power supply and case were already good picks, so they stay. The Core Ultra 9 285K costs about $190 more for the same 24 cores and similar gaming speed, so the 270K Plus is the better Intel buy.

Airflow

IN

The case’s own fans pull cool air across the board and GPU.

IN

Two Arctic P12 Pro fans push fresh air straight into the GPU. Keep the third as a spare.

OUT

Top: 360 mm radiator

CPU heat leaves through the roof and never warms the GPU.

OUT

Rear: 140 mm

More air comes in than goes out, which keeps dust out of the case.
